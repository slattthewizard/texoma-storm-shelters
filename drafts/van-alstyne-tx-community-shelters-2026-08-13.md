## Limitations of Public and Community Storm Shelters

While community spirit is strong in Van Alstyne, the practical limitations of public shelters often outweigh their theoretical benefits. These limitations are critical to understand when planning your family's severe weather safety:

*   **Distance and Travel Time:** Tornadoes can form rapidly. Driving even a few miles to a public shelter can expose you to dangerous conditions, including flying debris, heavy rain, and hail.
*   **Capacity Concerns:** Public shelters have finite space. During widespread emergencies, they can quickly reach capacity, potentially leaving late arrivals without a safe spot.
*   **Accessibility Issues:** Some public shelters may not be open 24/7 or might require staff to unlock and prepare them, causing delays during urgent situations.
*   **Pet Restrictions:** Many public shelters do not allow pets, forcing families to make agonizing choices or leave beloved animals behind.
*   **Lack of Privacy and Comfort:** Sharing a crowded space with strangers, often for extended periods, can be stressful and uncomfortable, especially for families with young children or individuals with specific needs.
*   **Property Protection:** A community shelter protects you, but it does nothing to safeguard your home or valuables. Your property remains vulnerable to damage while you are away.

If a tornado warning is issued with only a 10-minute lead time, the time it takes to gather your family, pets, and emergency kit, then drive to a potential community shelter, might exceed the available warning window.
