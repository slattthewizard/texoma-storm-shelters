## Choosing the Right Storm Shelter Type for New Construction

Selecting the appropriate storm shelter for your new home build depends on several factors, including available space, budget, personal preference, and the home's overall design. Texoma Storm Shelters offers various options perfectly suited for new construction projects.

### Above-Ground Safe Rooms

Above-ground safe rooms are an excellent choice for new homes, especially those without basements or where underground installation is not feasible due to soil conditions or water tables. These rooms are built within the home's footprint, often integrated into a garage, a closet, or a dedicated utility space. They are constructed with heavy-gauge steel and anchored directly to the concrete slab foundation, offering robust protection.

When planning a new home, an [above-ground safe room](/above-ground-safe-rooms/) can be designed as a permanent fixture, complete with ventilation, lighting, and power access. This allows it to serve multiple purposes during non-storm periods, such as a secure storage area or a panic room. Their placement above ground makes them highly accessible for all family members, including those with mobility issues.

### Underground Storm Shelters

For homeowners who prefer a discreet, out-of-sight solution, an [underground storm shelter](/underground-storm-shelters/) is a popular option. In new construction, these shelters can be installed with precision, ensuring proper drainage and structural integration. They are typically buried outside the home or beneath a garage slab.

A key advantage of integrating an underground shelter into a new build is the ability to prepare the excavation and foundation work simultaneously. This streamlines the construction process and ensures the shelter's entrance is optimally placed for convenient access while maintaining a low profile. These shelters are often made of steel or concrete, providing exceptional protection from extreme winds and debris.

### Garage Floor Shelters

[Garage floor shelters](/garage-floor-shelters/) offer the best of both worlds: discreet, underground protection with convenient access directly from your home. During new home construction, the installation of a garage floor shelter is particularly efficient. The shelter can be placed before the concrete slab is poured, allowing for perfect alignment and a flush finish with the garage floor.

This type of shelter is accessed via a heavy-duty steel lid that sits level with the garage floor, making it virtually invisible when closed. It provides immediate access to safety without stepping outside. For families in Sherman, Denison, or Whitesboro building new homes, a garage floor shelter is a highly practical and secure choice.

### Understanding Building Codes and Standards

In North Texas, storm shelter installations must meet specific building codes and recognized safety standards. The most important of these are FEMA P-320 (`https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/risk-management/safe-rooms/fema-p-320-guidelines`) and ICC-500. FEMA P-320 provides guidelines for constructing safe rooms, while ICC-500 (International Code Council 500) is the national standard for the design and construction of storm shelters.

These standards dictate everything from material strength and anchoring requirements to ventilation and door specifications.
