# Tornado Emergency vs PDS: What These Alerts Mean and What to Do

Most tornado warnings sound alike when they come through your phone. But a few carry stronger wording, and it can be confusing: tornado emergency, "particularly dangerous situation," PDS watch, PDS warning. What do they mean? And do you do anything differently?

Short answer: your action doesn't change. You shelter now. What changes is the message behind the alert, which is that the situation is as serious as these alerts get. Here's how the terms differ and how they reach you in Grayson, Fannin and Cooke counties.

## The standard alerts, quickly

Start with the basics, since the stronger wording builds on them.

- **Tornado watch.** Conditions are favorable for tornadoes in your area. It's issued by the Storm Prediction Center. Be ready.
- **Tornado warning.** A tornado is occurring or imminent, based on a sighting or radar. It's issued by your local National Weather Service office, which is Fort Worth for Grayson, Fannin and Cooke counties. Take shelter now.

If you want a refresher on those two, read [tornado watch vs warning](/blog/tornado-watch-vs-warning/). The stronger alerts below are layered on top of them.

## What is a tornado emergency?

A tornado emergency is the highest-severity tornado warning. Forecasters use it when a large, damaging tornado has been confirmed and is threatening a populated area. It isn't a separate kind of alert with a new action. It's a tornado warning with the strongest possible language attached, meant to jolt people who might otherwise delay.

The point is urgency. A tornado emergency tells you that a strong tornado is on the ground, that it's heading toward people, and that the stakes are high. If you hear one for your area, you shouldn't be deciding whether to shelter. You should already be moving.

## What does PDS mean?

PDS stands for "Particularly Dangerous Situation." It's wording used on some watches and warnings when forecasters see an unusually high-end setup. You might see it in a tornado watch, where it signals that conditions favor strong, long-lived tornadoes. You might see it in a warning too.

Two things to keep straight:

- A **PDS tornado watch** is still a watch. It says the setup is unusually dangerous and that you should be prepared well ahead of time. It's not a command to shelter this second.
- A **PDS tornado warning** is a warning with strong wording. A tornado is occurring or imminent, and the setup is unusually dangerous. You shelter now.

A tornado emergency is not simply a PDS warning under another name, so don't worry about memorizing exactly which phrase appears where. What matters is the escalation: watch, warning, then warnings with stronger wording as the threat increases.

## What to do differently

Nothing is different about where you go. The safest places are the same: a purpose-built shelter if you have one, or the lowest floor and an interior room away from windows if you don't. Our guide to [the safest place in a tornado](/blog/safest-place-in-a-tornado/) covers the options.

What changes is your mindset. A tornado emergency is the signal to stop delaying. That means:

- Don't step outside to look.
- Don't finish the errand or wait for a second alert.
- Don't try to film it or find the tornado on radar first.
- Get everyone, including pets, to shelter right now.
- Cover your head and neck.

If you're driving, the same guidance applies: get to a sturdy building if you can. See [what to do in a tornado while driving](/blog/what-to-do-tornado-while-driving/).

## How these alerts reach you

You can receive them several ways, and it helps to have more than one.

- **Wireless Emergency Alerts.** WEA pushes tornado warnings to phones in the warned area, with no app or sign-up. It's the fastest way for most people to hear. Learn more in [tornado alerts on your phone](/blog/tornado-alerts-on-your-phone/).
- **NOAA Weather Radio.** A radio keeps working when cell service doesn't, and you can program your SAME county code so it alerts only for your area: Grayson 048181, Fannin 048147, Cooke 048097, Collin 048085. Across the lake, Bryan is 040013, Marshall is 040095 and Love is 040085. See [NOAA Weather Radio in Texoma](/blog/noaa-weather-radio-texoma/).
- **Local TV and radio.** Broadcasters relay warnings and often track a storm's path in detail.
- **Outdoor sirens.** Sirens are meant for people outside. They may not wake you or be audible indoors, so don't rely on them alone.

Having a phone, a radio and a habit of checking the forecast covers most gaps. It's also worth knowing the day's outlook; our article on [severe weather risk categories](/blog/severe-weather-risk-categories/) explains the Storm Prediction Center's five levels.

## Why an upgraded alert is easy to miss

When people hear "tornado warning" many times a season, it's tempting to wait and see. That's exactly why forecasters reserve stronger wording for the worst cases. An emergency wording carries a simple message: this one is different.

Another reason is time. Tornadoes can move quickly, and a warning may give you only minutes. If your shelter is a closet in the middle of the house, you need that time to get there. If it's a safe room, you may have less to carry, but you still need to be inside with the door closed before the tornado arrives.

## What the strength of a tornado means

Tornadoes are rated on the Enhanced Fujita scale after the storm, based on damage surveys. EF0 covers winds of 65 to 85 mph and EF1 covers 86 to 110 mph. EF2 runs 111 to 135 mph, EF3 runs 136 to 165 mph, EF4 runs 166 to 200 mph, and EF5 is over 200 mph. These are 3-second gust estimates, and the rating comes after the fact, not while the warning is active. For the full scale, see [the Enhanced Fujita scale](/blog/enhanced-fujita-scale/).

A shelter built to FEMA P-320 criteria and tested to ICC 500 is designed for 250 mph winds, the design wind speed for all of North Texas. The debris test uses a 15 lb 2x4 at 100 mph. More on that in [ICC 500 safe room requirements](/blog/icc-500-safe-room-requirements/).

## Having somewhere to go

The hardest part of a high-end alert is realizing there's nowhere good to go. Many Texas homes have slab foundations and no basement, so there's no below-grade space. An [above-ground safe room](/above-ground-safe-rooms/), an [underground storm shelter](/underground-storm-shelters/) or a [garage floor shelter](/garage-floor-shelters/) gives you a defined place to go the moment an alert appears.

Installed in North Texas in 2026, a 4-6 person above-ground steel safe room runs about $4,500 to $6,200, and an 8-10 person unit runs $6,500 to $8,200. Garage in-floor shelters run $4,800 to $7,900. The typical residential range is $4,500 to $9,500, and the [storm shelter cost](/storm-shelter-cost/) page has the breakdown. Grayson County's safe room rebate covers 50% of cost up to $3,000 when it's open, requires pre-approval before installation, and runs periodically; if you're outside Grayson County, ask your county emergency management office whether a program is open.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a tornado emergency?

A tornado emergency is the highest-severity tornado warning. It's used when a large, damaging tornado is confirmed and threatening a populated area. You respond the way you would to any warning, by taking shelter immediately, but the wording tells you not to delay.

### What does PDS mean on a tornado watch or warning?

PDS stands for Particularly Dangerous Situation. It's wording used on some watches and warnings when forecasters see an unusually high-end setup. A PDS watch means prepare early. A PDS warning means a tornado is occurring or imminent and you should shelter now.

### Is a tornado emergency different from a regular tornado warning?

The action is the same: shelter now. The difference is the confirmed threat. An emergency means a large, damaging tornado is heading toward a populated area, which is the signal to stop any delay.

### Do I do anything different for a PDS alert?

Not in where you go or how you protect yourself. Go to your shelter or the safest interior spot on the lowest floor, away from windows, and cover your head and neck. The difference is that you should prepare earlier for a PDS watch and not wait around for a PDS warning.

### How will I find out about a tornado emergency?

Wireless Emergency Alerts send tornado warnings to phones in the warned area with no app or sign-up. A NOAA Weather Radio, local TV and radio, and outdoor sirens can also help. Use more than one, since any single method can fail.

### Should I leave the area during a tornado emergency?

Usually no. Moving by car during a warning is dangerous, because tornadoes can change direction and roads fill with traffic. Take shelter where you are, in the sturdiest place available. If you're already on the road, get to the nearest sturdy building.
