# The Best Time to Install a Storm Shelter in North Texas

Most people start looking at storm shelters right after a bad storm. A tornado touches down somewhere nearby, the news coverage runs for days, and suddenly everyone wants one. That's understandable, and it's also the worst moment to be shopping.

If you're reading this in late fall or winter, you're in a good position. Here's why the cold months are usually the best time to install a storm shelter in Texoma, what affects the schedule, and what to do if spring has already arrived.

## Why fall and winter work best

North Texas has tornadoes in every month. Spring, roughly March through June, is the main peak, and there's a smaller bump in the fall. So there's no truly "safe" season, but there is a quiet stretch between the fall bump and the spring ramp-up. That's the window when installing a shelter is easiest for everyone.

Several things line up in your favor:

- **You finish before the peak.** A shelter installed in January is ready when the first spring outlooks appear. A shelter you order in April may not be.
- **Less pressure on the schedule.** Installers tend to be busiest when storms are in the news. Off-season, there's more room to fit your site visit, your paperwork, and your install date without rushing.
- **More time to compare.** You can get multiple estimates, ask follow-up questions, and read the fine print without feeling like a storm is coming next week. Our guide to [hiring a storm shelter contractor](/blog/hiring-storm-shelter-contractor/) has questions worth bringing.
- **Time to handle permits and pre-approvals.** Paperwork takes the time it takes. Starting in winter means those steps don't have to be squeezed.

None of this means you should wait if you're ready now. If you're deciding in October, don't hold out for January. The point is that the cold months are a good time to move, not the only time.

## Demand spikes after storms in the news

When a major tornado hits somewhere in the region, a lot of people who were on the fence decide at the same moment. Schedules can tighten fast, and the weeks after a high-profile storm are not the best time to be at the back of a long list.

There's another reason to avoid buying in a rush. Decisions made under fear tend to skip steps. People sign the first quote, skip comparing shelter types, or don't ask about testing and warranties. Making the decision in a calm month lets you choose between an [above-ground safe room](/above-ground-safe-rooms/), an [underground storm shelter](/underground-storm-shelters/), and a [garage floor shelter](/garage-floor-shelters/) based on your site and budget instead of urgency. Our article on [above-ground versus underground shelters](/blog/above-ground-vs-underground-shelter/) can help you sort that out.

## Rebate timing: pre-approval comes first

If you're in Grayson County, the OEM safe room rebate can cover 50% of the cost up to $3,000, funded through FEMA hazard mitigation grants. A few details matter for timing:

- **It runs periodically.** It isn't always open, so you shouldn't assume it's available right now. Check the current status with the county.
- **Pre-approval is required before installation.** If the shelter goes in first, you're generally out of luck. That means the rebate paperwork is the first step, not the last.
- **Eligibility rules apply.** The home needs to be outside the floodplain, and the shelter must meet FEMA P-320 and ICC 500.

Because approval takes time and has to come before work starts, a quiet season is a sensible time to apply. If you're outside Grayson County, ask your county emergency management office whether a program is open. Don't assume one exists or doesn't. Our guide to the [Grayson County storm shelter rebate](/blog/grayson-county-storm-shelter-rebate/) explains the process step by step.

## Ground conditions: wet clay versus dry clay

Weather affects the install itself, especially for underground or in-floor shelters that require excavation. A few things to know:

**Blackland Prairie clay.** Much of Grayson County sits on clay that shrinks and swells with moisture. When it's saturated, it's heavy, sticky, and hard to work with. When it's dry and cracked, it can be hard to dig and may shift as the moisture changes. Neither extreme is ideal, and the right timing depends on what the ground is doing the week of your install.

**Cooke County and Cross Timbers.** Cooke County is mostly Cross Timbers country, which brings its own soil and rock conditions. Ask your installer what they expect on your specific lot.

**Wet weather and delays.** After a heavy rain, an excavation can fill with water, and an installer may need to wait for the site to dry. If you're installing an underground unit, ask how they handle drainage during and after the dig. Our article on [water in a storm shelter](/blog/water-in-storm-shelter/) explains why this matters over the long run.

**Above-ground units.** These need a properly prepared slab and anchoring, so they depend less on digging conditions but still need the concrete to cure before the shelter is set. Ask about the schedule.

The honest answer is that no month is perfect in clay country. Winter can be wet, summer can be dry and hard, and spring brings storms. That's one more reason to talk with the installer about conditions on your property rather than guessing from the calendar.

## Lead times vary, so ask

How long it takes from signing to a finished shelter depends on a lot: the type of shelter, whether it's built to order, how busy the installer is, whether a permit or rebate approval is involved, and even the weather. We can't give you a single number that would be true for everyone, and anyone who promises one without seeing your site is guessing.

What you can do is ask a few direct questions:

- How long from signing to installation, and what's that based on?
- Is the shelter built to order, or stocked? Does that change the timeline?
- What steps come before install, such as permits, utility locates, or rebate approval?
- What happens if weather delays the dig or the pour?
- Will the date be in writing?

Our overview of the [storm shelter installation process](/blog/storm-shelter-installation-process/) walks through the stages from site visit to final walkthrough, so you know what each step involves. For permit questions, check with your city or county building department, and read our guide to [storm shelter permits in Texas](/blog/storm-shelter-permits-texas/).

## A simple timeline to work backward from

Here's a way to think about it if your goal is to be done before spring storm season:

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Fall | Decide what type of shelter fits your site. Get estimates. Check on rebate status |
| Early winter | If a rebate is open, apply and wait for pre-approval. Sign a written estimate once you've compared options |
| Mid-winter | Handle permits, site prep, and scheduling. Confirm the install date in writing |
| Late winter | Installation, inspection, and a walkthrough. Register the shelter |
| Before spring | Stock the shelter, run a family drill, and know your alerts |

Treat these as a rough order of steps, not a promise about how long each takes. Yours may move faster or slower depending on the factors above.

Once the shelter is in, don't skip the finishing touches. Our guides on [registering your storm shelter](/blog/register-your-storm-shelter/), [storm shelter supplies](/blog/storm-shelter-supplies/), and [storm shelter prep](/blog/storm-shelter-prep/) cover what to do after the crew leaves.

## What to do if it's already spring

Maybe you're reading this in April, and you don't want to wait until next year. That's fair. Waiting through the peak without a shelter carries its own risk, so don't let the calendar talk you out of acting.

Here's what makes sense:

1. **Call now and ask for current availability.** Get an honest answer on timing, in writing, and don't assume the earliest date is the one you'll get.
2. **Make a stopgap plan.** Until the shelter is installed, know your best interior room and keep your supplies by the door. Read our guide on the [safest place in a tornado](/blog/safest-place-in-a-tornado/).
3. **Set up your alerts.** Wireless Emergency Alerts push tornado warnings to your phone with no app, and a NOAA Weather Radio is a good backup. See our [guide to NOAA Weather Radio in Texoma](/blog/noaa-weather-radio-texoma/).
4. **Check on a rebate, but don't let it hold you up.** If you can't get approval before installing, you may have to choose between waiting and paying full price. Decide what matters more to your household.
5. **Don't skip comparison.** Even in a rush, get at least two written estimates and ask about testing, anchoring, and warranty.

Prices depend mainly on the type of shelter. Installed prices in North Texas run $4,500 to $6,200 for a 4-6 person above-ground unit, $6,500 to $8,200 for 8-10 people, and $5,200 to $8,800 for underground units depending on design. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is winter a good time to install a storm shelter?

Usually, yes. It's ahead of the spring peak, schedules tend to be less crowded, and you have time for permits, comparisons, and any rebate pre-approval. Wet ground can slow some digs, so ask your installer how they handle it.

### Should I wait for the Grayson County rebate before installing?

Only if you're comfortable with the wait. The rebate runs periodically and requires pre-approval before installation, so a shelter installed first generally won't qualify. Check with the county on current status, and decide whether the possible savings are worth the delay for your household.

### How long does a storm shelter installation take?

It depends on the shelter type, your site, permits, scheduling, and weather, so ask your installer for a written timeline based on your property. Don't rely on a generic number from anyone who hasn't seen the site.

### Does weather affect when a shelter can be installed?

Yes, especially for underground and in-floor units that require excavation. Heavy rain can flood a dig, and Blackland Prairie clay can be hard to work when it's soaked or baked dry. Ask how the installer handles those conditions and what happens to your schedule if the weather turns.

### Can I install a storm shelter during tornado season?

You can, but expect busier schedules and more demand, especially after storms make the news. Ask for current availability in writing, and set up a stopgap plan and alerts for your household until the shelter is ready.

### Do I need a permit before installing a storm shelter?

Requirements vary, so check with your city or county building department before work starts. Permit steps can affect your timeline, which is another reason to start early in the off-season rather than the week a storm is forecast.
