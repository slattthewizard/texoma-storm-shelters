## Types of Storm Shelters and Their Investment Considerations

Texoma Storm Shelters offers a range of options, each with distinct advantages and investment profiles. Understanding these types helps you choose the best fit for your home and budget. The primary types include above-ground safe rooms, underground storm shelters, and garage floor shelters. Each option provides certified protection but varies in installation, accessibility, and footprint.

Above-ground safe rooms are typically installed inside an existing structure, such as a garage or a dedicated room. They are highly accessible, especially for individuals with mobility challenges, and can often be customized to blend with your home's aesthetics. Their installation process is generally less intrusive than underground options. You can learn more about these robust structures on our /above-ground-safe-rooms/ page.

Underground storm shelters are buried beneath the ground, often in the yard or near the home's foundation. They offer excellent protection and are out of sight, preserving yard space. These shelters require excavation, which impacts the installation cost and timeline. They are a classic choice for maximum protection and can be explored further on our /underground-storm-shelters/ page.

Garage floor shelters are installed flush with your garage floor, hidden beneath a steel lid. They are discreet, easily accessible from within your home, and do not consume valuable garage space when not in use. This type is popular for its convenience and minimal visual impact. Discover more about this practical solution at /garage-floor-shelters/. Each type represents a solid storm shelter investment, differing primarily in user experience and installation specifics.

### What are the key safety standards for storm shelters?
The primary safety standards for storm shelters are outlined by FEMA P-320 (Taking Shelter from the Storm: Building a Safe Room for Your Home or Small Business) and ICC-500 (Standard for the Design and Construction of Storm Shelters). These standards cover structural integrity, material strength, anchorage, ventilation, and impact resistance to ensure the shelter can withstand EF5 tornado forces and flying debris.
